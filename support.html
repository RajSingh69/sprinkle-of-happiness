Compassion over condemnation

Suicide is not a moral failure.

Suicidal thoughts are not evidence of weakness. People can reach points of overwhelming distress, hopelessness, isolation or pain, and shame can make it harder to ask for help.

We want to create spaces where people can speak before a crisis, be heard without judgement and reconnect with reasons to stay.

Faith and compassion

While we are here, we can care for one another.

People hold many different beliefs about life, death, spirituality and what may come afterwards. Some religious language can increase shame for someone already suffering. Sprinkle of Happiness chooses compassion, listening, connection and care over condemnation.

This is connected to Seva, compassion, equality and Chardi Kala.

What we believe

Suicide prevention can include standing beside people.

It can include listening, reducing isolation, practical support, helping someone connect with professional support, noticing when somebody is struggling, honest conversations, creative expression and community connection.

Sprinkle of Happiness workshops, circles and conversations are not emergency services, therapy or clinical treatment.

Bereavement

There is no single correct way to mourn.

Grief after suicide can include shock, anger, guilt, confusion, love and unanswered questions. Sprinkle aims to make room for remembrance, honest conversation and compassion.

You do not have to solve your whole life tonight.

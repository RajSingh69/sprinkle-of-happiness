Our Curriculum

Learning to Live, Create, Connect and Flourish

At Sprinkle of Happiness, learning is more than acquiring knowledge. It is about discovering ourselves, exploring our experiences, building meaningful connections and finding new possibilities for how we live.

Our curriculum brings together educational ideas, reflective practice, creativity, community and Sikhi-inspired values. It is a guiding philosophy for how we design our spaces, not an accredited course.

The Four Pillars of Learning

Our approach draws inspiration from UNESCO's Four Pillars of Learning, set out in the report Learning: The Treasure Within (1996). Here is how we interpret each pillar in our own work.

Pillar one

Learning to Know

Exploring ourselves, our experiences and the world around us.

  • Self-awareness
  • Curiosity
  • Reflection
  • Understanding emotions
  • Exploring new perspectives
  • Lifelong learning
Pillar two

Learning to Do

Putting knowledge and reflection into practice.

  • Creative expression
  • Journalling
  • Practical skills
  • Trying new experiences
  • Building confidence through participation
  • Turning ideas into action
Pillar three

Learning to Live Together

Learning through relationships, understanding and community.

  • Empathy
  • Respect
  • Belonging
  • Listening
  • Collaboration
  • Appreciating difference
  • Shared experiences
Pillar four

Learning to Be

Developing as a whole person.

  • Identity
  • Values
  • Creativity
  • Self-expression
  • Personal growth
  • Meaning
  • Agency and possibility

Sprinkle of Happiness is not affiliated with, approved or endorsed by UNESCO. The pillars are an inspiration for our thinking.

Sikhi-rooted values

Rooted in Values, Open to Everyone

Sikhi inspires how we think about dignity, compassion, service and community. These values shape how we welcome and care for people. Nobody is asked to practise Sikhi or hold any religious belief, and this is never religious instruction.

  • SevaSelfless service: contributing to others without expecting anything back.
  • Sarbat da BhalaWishing for the wellbeing of all people, everywhere.
  • Chardi KalaResilient, hopeful, rising spirits, even in hard times.
  • Equality and dignityRecognising the equal worth of every person.
  • SangatThe strength of a supportive community gathered together.

Our roots shape how we welcome people. They do not determine who is allowed to belong.

People of all faiths and none, and people of every background, are welcome.

Reflective practice

Understanding Ourselves Through Reflection

Our activities draw on ideas from psychology, learning, emotional awareness and reflective practice. Writing, conversation, creativity and movement can give people room to notice what they think and feel, and to make their own meaning from it.

These activities are for learning, creativity and wellbeing. They are not a substitute for healthcare or psychological treatment.

Shadow journalling

Shadow Journalling: Exploring What We Carry

Shadow journalling is a reflective writing practice for gently exploring the thoughts, feelings, beliefs, memories and patterns we might find difficult to acknowledge. It is a creative practice, not therapy or trauma processing.

Possible themes

  • Identity and belonging
  • Fear and shame
  • Expectations and pressure
  • Relationships and boundaries
  • Hope and imagination
  • Personal strengths
  • Patterns and choices

Example prompts

  • What parts of myself do I find difficult to express?
  • Whose expectations am I carrying?
  • When do I feel most like myself?
  • What does belonging mean to me?
  • What would I like to give myself permission to explore?

Journalling is always optional, and you can skip any question. Please don't revisit overwhelming or traumatic experiences without appropriate professional support. If you need help now, visit Need Support?

The Sprinkle of Happiness journey

Five stages, in whatever order life brings them.

The journey is a way of describing what our spaces make room for. People may revisit stages, live several at once, or connect with the journey in their own way. There is no fixed order and no finish line.

  • Survival

    Acknowledging where we are, recognising challenges and creating room for support.

  • Expression

    Exploring feelings, ideas and identity through writing, art, movement and conversation.

  • Connection

    Finding belonging through relationships, community and shared experiences.

  • Hope

    Exploring possibility, imagination and meaningful future directions.

  • Joy

    Making space for creativity, celebration, gratitude, play and Chardi Kala.
